# Module 1 — Video Narration Script

> A companion script for filming or voicing-over Module 1.
> Each slide gets a ~30-second narration. Total runtime: ~12–14 minutes.
> Tone: warm, slow, reassuring. Imagine speaking to your father directly.
> Pause at every comma. Keep sentences short.

---

## Production notes

- **Pacing:** ~120 words per minute. If a section feels short, slow down.
- **Visual cues:** mention what's on screen (`[shows Finder sidebar]`)
  so the editor matches narration to the screen recording.
- **Bengali asides:** the indented Bengali lines are optional voiceover
  inserts — record them as separate clips so they can be toggled.
- **Music:** none. Quiet room. Narration only.
- **B-roll opportunities:** flagged with `[B-ROLL]` — short cutaway shots.

---

## Part A — Welcome & Mental Model

### Slide 1 — Welcome (~30s)

> Hello, Dad. This is your Mac.
>
> Everything you knew on Windows is still here. The names changed. Some buttons moved.
> The keyboard works a little differently. That's it.
>
> Read at your own pace. Re-read anything you forget. There is no test, no time limit.
>
> [B-ROLL: hand on trackpad, MacBook on desk, calm]

> _Bengali insert: ভয় পেও না। Windows-এ যা পারতে, ম্যাকেও সবই পারবে।_

---

### Slide 2 — Rosetta Table (~35s)

> Same things — new names. That's the trick to switching from Windows to Mac.
>
> Here are the eight names you will hear most often.
>
> File Explorer is now called Finder.
> The Taskbar is now called the Dock.
> The Start Menu is now called Spotlight.
> The Recycle Bin is now called the Trash.
>
> Don't try to memorize. Just glance at this table whenever you forget a word.
>
> [shows table on screen, slow zoom on each row]

---

### Slide 3 — The Four Corners (~40s)

> Anywhere on your Mac, you can always find these four areas.
>
> At the top of the screen — the Menu Bar.
> At the bottom — the Dock, with all your favourite apps.
> In the middle — the Desktop, which is just empty space for files.
> And the Finder — the smiley-face icon that opens up all your folders.
>
> Once you know these four corners, you'll never feel lost.
>
> [B-ROLL: cursor circling each of the four areas]

> _Bengali insert: চারটে জায়গা — Menu Bar, Dock, Desktop, Finder।_

---

### Slide 4 — ⌘ replaces Ctrl (~35s)

> This is the single biggest change. On Windows, you held Control for shortcuts.
> On Mac, you hold Command. The Command key has a cloverleaf symbol on it,
> and it's right next to the spacebar.
>
> Copy is now Command-C.
> Paste is Command-V.
> Save is Command-S.
> Undo is Command-Z.
>
> Your fingers will learn this in a week. Don't worry about memorizing.
>
> [shows close-up of the ⌘ key on the MacBook keyboard]

> _Bengali insert: Windows-এ Ctrl, ম্যাকে এখন ⌘ — বাকি সব এক।_

---

### Slide 5 — ⌘+Space, Spotlight (~40s)

> When in doubt, press Command-Space.
>
> Anywhere on your Mac, hold Command and press Space. A search bar opens in
> the middle of the screen. Type the name of an app, a file, or a setting.
> Press Enter. Done.
>
> You can even ask it for quick maths. Type "two-fifty times twelve" — the
> answer appears instantly.
>
> If you only remember one shortcut from this whole guide, remember this one:
> Command-Space is your universal escape hatch.
>
> [B-ROLL: hand pressing ⌘+Space, search bar appears, typing "WhatsApp"]

## Part B — Filesystem Geography

### Slide 6 — Open Finder (~25s)

> Time to meet Finder. Finder is the Mac's File Explorer.
>
> Look at the bottom of the screen — the Dock. The first icon is a
> half-blue, half-grey smiley face. That's Finder. Click it. A window opens.
>
> This is where you'll see every file and every folder on your Mac.

---

### Slide 7 — The Sidebar (~40s)

> On the left side of the Finder window, you'll see a list of locations.
> Five of them matter for what you'll do every day.
>
> Your name — that's your Home folder.
> Desktop — what you see on your wallpaper.
> Documents — for your important Word and Excel files.
> Downloads — where everything from the internet lands.
> And Applications — the home of all your apps.
>
> The other entries you can ignore. Just these five.

> _Bengali insert: পাঁচটা জায়গা — Home, Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Applications।_

---

### Slide 8 — Where Downloads Go (~35s) — CRITICAL SLIDE

> This is the most important slide in this module. Listen carefully.
>
> When you save a file from WhatsApp, from your email, or from a website —
> by default, it goes to one place. The Downloads folder.
>
> Not the Desktop. Not Documents. Not anywhere else. The Downloads folder.
>
> If you ever can't find a file you just saved — open Finder, click Downloads
> on the left, and there it is at the top.
>
> [shows arrow drawing from "Save" button to Downloads folder in sidebar]

> _Bengali insert: WhatsApp বা ইমেইল থেকে কিছু সেভ করলে — সব Downloads ফোল্ডারে যায়। অন্য কোথাও খুঁজো না।_

---

### Slide 9 — Quick Look (~30s)

> Mac has a magic feature called Quick Look.
>
> Click any file once. Just one click — don't double-click. Then press the
> Space bar. A preview pops up showing what's inside the file.
>
> Press Space again. The preview closes.
>
> No app opened. No waiting. This works for photos, PDFs, Word documents,
> Excel sheets, even videos. Use it constantly.
>
> [B-ROLL: clicking on PDF in Finder, pressing Space, preview appears]

---

### Slide 10 — Spotlight to find files (~30s)

> Even if you don't remember which folder you saved a file in, Spotlight will find it.
>
> Press Command-Space. Type any part of the name. The file appears in the results.
> Click it to open, or hit Enter.
>
> If you want to know <em>where</em> the file lives — hold Command while clicking
> the result. Spotlight reveals the folder.

---

### Slide 11 — Scroll Direction (~40s) — CRITICAL SLIDE

> Here's the second-biggest surprise. Mac scrolls "naturally" — which is the
> opposite of Windows. When you slide two fingers down on the trackpad,
> the page goes up. Many Windows users find this confusing.
>
> Here's how to switch it back to Windows-style:
>
> Click the Apple menu — the black apple at the top-left.
> Choose System Settings. In the sidebar, click Trackpad.
> Click the Scroll & Zoom tab. Find "Natural scrolling" and turn it off.
>
> Try it both ways before deciding. Some people like the Mac default.
>
> [shows step-by-step screen recording of System Settings]

> _Bengali insert: ম্যাকে স্ক্রল উল্টো লাগে। ঠিক করতে — Apple → System Settings → Trackpad → Natural scrolling বন্ধ করো।_

## Part C — The Workflow

### Slide 12 — The Big Picture (~30s)

> Here's the workflow you'll use every single day.
>
> A message arrives on WhatsApp. You save the file to your Mac. You drop it
> into ATLAS. ATLAS gives you an answer. You copy what's useful. You send it
> back — by email, or back into WhatsApp.
>
> Five steps. Two minutes once you've done it twice. Let's go through each one.

---

### Slide 13 — Open WhatsApp (~25s)

> Two ways to open WhatsApp on your Mac.
>
> One: click the green WhatsApp icon in the Dock at the bottom of the screen.
>
> Two: press Command-Space, type WhatsApp, press Enter.
>
> Use whichever you like. The second is usually faster.

---

### Slide 14 — Save the File (~40s)

> A photo arrived on WhatsApp. You want to save it to your Mac.
>
> Right-click the photo. Right-click on Mac means tap with two fingers on the
> trackpad — not one. Or hold Control and click.
>
> A menu appears. Choose "Save Image As". A dialog opens. The default save
> location is Downloads. Click Save.
>
> Done. Your photo is now on your Mac, in the Downloads folder.
>
> For documents, click the message, then click the small download arrow in
> the corner. It saves to Downloads too.

> _Bengali insert: WhatsApp থেকে সব ফাইল ও ছবি Downloads-এ যায়।_

---

### Slide 15 — Confirm It Saved (~25s)

> Let's confirm the file actually saved.
>
> Open Finder. Click Downloads on the left. Your file is at the top —
> the most recent files appear first.
>
> Click it once. Press Space. A preview opens. You can see the photo or
> document. Press Space again to close.
>
> Confirmed. You're ready for the next step.

---

### Slide 16 — Open ATLAS (~25s)

> ATLAS is your AI browser. It looks like Safari or Chrome, but you can ask
> it questions and it answers — using ChatGPT.
>
> Click the ATLAS icon in the Dock. Or press Command-Space, type ATLAS,
> press Enter.
>
> ATLAS opens with a chat box ready for you.

---

### Slide 17 — Send to ATLAS (~45s)

> Two ways to give ATLAS your file or text.
>
> The first is dragging. Open Finder, find your file, click and hold,
> drag it across the screen, and drop it on the ATLAS chat box. ATLAS
> uploads the file.
>
> The second is pasting. Open the file by double-clicking it.
> Press Command-A to select everything. Command-C to copy.
> Click in the ATLAS chat box. Command-V to paste.
>
> Use whichever works. Dragging is good for whole documents. Pasting is
> good when you only want part of the text.

> _Bengali insert: টানো, বা কপি-পেস্ট করো — দু'টোই ঠিক আছে।_

---

### Slide 18 — Ask Your Question (~40s)

> Type your question. Press Enter.
>
> A few questions that work well: "Summarize this in five bullet points."
> "Translate this letter into Bengali." "Explain this contract in simple words."
>
> Tips for better answers. Be specific — "summarize" is good, "summarize in
> five bullet points" is better. Tell ATLAS who it's writing for. And if you
> don't like the answer, just type "make it shorter" — it tries again.
>
> ATLAS is patient. It won't get tired of you asking.

---

### Slide 19 — Copy the Answer (~30s)

> Most ATLAS answers have a small Copy button at the bottom of the response.
> Click it. Done — the answer is on your clipboard.
>
> If there's no Copy button, click at the start of the text, hold and drag
> to the end. The text turns blue. Then Command-C.
>
> To test the copy worked, open TextEdit and press Command-V. If your text
> appears, you're good. Close TextEdit without saving.

---

### Slide 20 — Paste into Email (~40s)

> Now send the answer by email.
>
> Open Gmail in your browser, or open the Mail app. Click Compose. Fill in
> who it's going to and what the subject is.
>
> Click in the message body. Press Command-V. The ATLAS answer pastes in.
>
> Click Send.
>
> Want to attach the original file too? Drag it from Finder Downloads
> into the email body. It attaches automatically.

---

### Slide 21 — Paste into WhatsApp (~35s)

> Or send the answer back to the same WhatsApp chat.
>
> Open WhatsApp. Click the chat. Click in the message box at the bottom.
> Press Command-V. The text pastes in. Press Enter to send.
>
> To send the file along with the message, click the plus icon, choose
> Document or Photo, pick the file from Downloads.
>
> That's the full loop. WhatsApp to Mac to ATLAS to Mac to WhatsApp.
> You did it.

> _Bengali insert: পুরো চক্র — WhatsApp থেকে ম্যাকে, ATLAS-এ, আবার ফিরে। তুমি করতে পারলে।_

---

## Part D — Recovery & Safety

### Slide 22 — Lost File (~30s)

> Three ways to find a file you've lost.
>
> One: press Command-Space, type any part of the name, the file appears.
>
> Two: open Finder, click Recents — you'll see every file you've recently
> opened or saved.
>
> Three: check Downloads. If you saved from WhatsApp, email, or the web,
> it's there.

---

### Slide 23 — ⌘+Z Panic Button (~30s)

> Whenever something goes wrong, press Command-Z.
>
> Typed something wrong? Command-Z. Deleted a paragraph? Command-Z.
> Moved a file to the wrong folder? Command-Z. Press it more than once
> to keep going back.
>
> If you closed a browser tab by mistake — Command-Shift-T brings it back.

---

### Slide 24 — Force Quit (~35s)

> An app froze. The cursor became a spinning rainbow. Don't restart the Mac.
>
> Press Command-Option-Escape — three keys at once. A small window opens
> with all your apps. Click the stuck one. Click Force Quit. Confirm.
>
> The app closes. Other apps keep running. You're back to normal.
>
> This is the Mac's version of Control-Alt-Delete plus End Task — but in
> one shortcut.

> _Bengali insert: অ্যাপ আটকে গেলে — Command-Option-Escape, Force Quit।_

---

### Slide 25 — Privacy & Safety (~50s) — CRITICAL SLIDE

> Last slide of the module. Listen carefully.
>
> AI tools are useful, but anything you paste into them may be stored on
> someone else's computer. Treat them like a public notice board.
>
> Never paste these into ChatGPT, ATLAS, Claude, or Gemini.
> Passwords or PINs. Bank account numbers, credit card numbers, OTPs.
> Your NID, passport, or any government ID number. Medical reports with your
> name on them. Other people's private details.
>
> What's safe to share? Letters and emails without sensitive numbers.
> News articles. Recipes. General questions. Documents where you've removed
> personal information first.
>
> If you ever paste something private by mistake — tell your son immediately.
> Change the password. Call the bank. Don't panic, but treat it as if everyone
> could see it.

> _Bengali insert: পাসওয়ার্ড, ব্যাংক একাউন্ট, ক্রেডিট কার্ড, NID — কখনোই AI-কে দিও না।_

### After Slide 25 — Module Wrap (~25s)

> Module 1 is done.
>
> You can now open WhatsApp, save a file to your Mac, find it in Downloads,
> drop it into ATLAS, get an answer, and send it back by email or WhatsApp.
>
> If you forget any of this — open the guide. The Cheat Sheet has every

>
> Take a break. The next module will teach the universal moves — copy,
